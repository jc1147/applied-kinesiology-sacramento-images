Anatomy you can see, in a real clinic

The direction you picked: a real clinic, a glowing teal anatomy overlay of the structure each page talks about, and a soft red glow where it hurts, with the treatment aimed right at it. It's mixed with the illustration plates. 36 overlay photos, 8 clinic photos and 13 plates across 35 pages. The photos are Higgsfield (Marketing Studio 2.5 Flare); 10 plates were redrawn there, and 3 are kept from the first set (fal).

A real room behind every shot

Every photo is set in a real clinic (oak shelves, a window, equipment) with the room softly out of focus. No plain backdrops.

Red where it hurts

Every overlay photo has a soft red-orange glow at the painful structure, and the hands, laser, PEMF coil or adjusting instrument work right on it.

Hands reviewed

Independent reviewers zoomed in and counted every finger on every hand, and checked every body was whole and every overlay was the right way round: 258 hands counted across both rounds.

Photos and plates, mixed

The bone-only plates sit alongside the photos. The ones you liked stay; the ones with anatomy a chiropractor would catch were redrawn.

Review, round by round

  • First pass: 24 of 45 photos failed. 20 had an overlay error (drawn upside down, shoulder blades on the lower back, extra vertebrae) and 13 a hand or body error (a third leg, a four-fingered hand, six toes). Plate redraws: 10 of 20 failed.
  • Round 2: 22 failed shots and 2 plates re-rendered twice each with tighter prompts, then reviewed again: 8 pass, 17 minor, 23 fail across the variants; the better one of each pair went in.
  • Round 3: 4 clinic shots redone (people had appeared in scenes meant to be empty, a three-fingered hand, a melted phone keypad), and 8 treatment shots were re-shot from a new angle: 5 that had failed twice, and 3 whose round-2 version had a flaw worth fixing (a spine off the body's axis, a foot with no heel bone, a crop that cut off the head). The fixes: face-down patients shot from the foot end, where the spine overlay lands right; one hand on the patient instead of a reach that grew a third arm; one foot seen from the side; a wider frame so the page crop keeps the head and toes. Reviewed again: 1 pass, 11 minor, 8 fail across the 20 variants; the best of each shot went in.
  • In the set now: 19 pass, 38 with minor notes, 0 failed. Each image shows its result; open "Review notes" for the details.

At full scale Flare still followed the poses, framing and devices, and that's why every photo here comes from it. The first pass failure rate (24 of 45) is higher than the first set's (11 of 47), because every photo now has to get the anatomy overlay right as well. Hands and overlays still need a reviewer; no model gets them right every time. The full test: compare/page/Photo-Direction-Test.html.

Cost

  • fal: first set $12.39, plus $1.14 for its half of the test.
  • Higgsfield: at least 157 Flare renders (every variant and redo, including the test), plus 12 Soul and Soul V2 test renders.
  • Flare is billed by tokens and the API doesn't return the charge. At my estimate of $0.20–0.30 per image that's about $31–47. That figure is unverified; your Higgsfield console has the exact spend, and every render's request ID is saved next to it.
